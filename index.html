How a plate travels

Three people, one short chain: a donor posts, an NGO requests, a volunteer collects. Every step is timestamped so both sides know exactly where the food is.

Before you post

  • Keep cooked food covered and below 5 °C or above 60 °C until pickup.
  • Post a realistic window — most NGOs need 90 minutes to reach you.
  • Count in meals, not vessels, so the NGO can plan distribution.
  • Never list food that has already been served at a table.

Why AnnaSetu exists

India wastes roughly a third of the food it prepares while millions go to bed hungry. The gap is rarely supply — it is the missing few kilometres and the missing few hours between a full vessel and an empty plate. AnnaSetu is that bridge.

Small habits that cut waste

  • Plan meals and portion counts before you cook for a crowd.
  • Store cooked food properly the moment service ends.
  • Share surplus the same day instead of refrigerating it twice.
  • Donate first, discard only as a last resort.

Who uses the platform

Restaurants and cloud kitchens after a slow evening. College messes and hostels during holidays. Function halls after weddings. Households after a festival. On the other side: registered NGOs, shelter homes and volunteer groups who can collect and distribute within hours.

Small actions add up. Share food, spread hope.
